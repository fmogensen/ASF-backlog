The spec's *The design* §2. This is the half the card was filed for: the `in-flight` rule reads
`branch or pr_state` and never `open_prs` (P3), the queue had already deleted the branch (P17), so
`pr_state` was the entire hold — and `pr_state` came from `next((p for p in cand_prs if
p.get("state") != "CLOSED"), None)`, which asks the PR list and nothing else (P4). A list read
before the landing says OPEN, and the card reads `PR #1046 OPEN / rule: in-flight` for 40 min.

One pure `chosen_pr(cand_prs, merged, landed)` returns the row's three PR fields together (C6),
because the three are one decision and today they are three coupled expressions inside two nested
loops that a reviewer cannot run. The order is: a candidate the list says MERGED, at its `merged`
sha; then one the trunk carries as landed, read as `MERGED` at the trunk's sha whatever the list
says (C8 — "a cached OPEN row is a copy of an older answer; the trunk is the answer"); then the
first candidate that is not CLOSED, at its own state.

It is read as MERGED *with* `merged_sha`, not merely dropped from `pr_state` (C5). Clearing
`pr_state` alone would take the Task from wrongly-Active to wrongly-**New** whenever `id_evidence`
did not also match the trunk commit to the id; `merged_sha` is the field `landed-green` reads
(`_landed(ev) = bool(ev.commit or ev.merged_sha)`, PD7), and it is what turns ingest's line into
`PR #1046 merged (bc62d611)` with `green` set (PD6). No rule and no rule order changes.
