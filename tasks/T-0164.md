stories: S-17406, S-17407

§2.6 and §2.7 — the pass itself, and the cadence the card asks for. Last, because it drives every
other Task's code, and because §3.6's fence names classes from three modules and can only be run
whole once they all exist (PD15).

**Files**

- `asf/savings/savings.py` — **new**: `cmd_savings(args, root)`, the per-kind table, the class table
  and the re-measure pass.
- `asf/cli.py` — `p_savings = sub.add_parser('savings', …)` with `--day`, `--window`, `--json`,
  `--dry-run`, and the dispatch through `resolve_record`, exactly as `file-bugs` is registered
  (`:100`).
- `asf/tick/step_daily.py` — `parts()` (`:154`) gains the `savings` entry immediately before
  `rollup` (PD9).
- `tests/test_savings.py` — `CommandTests` (new, §3.6).
- `tests/test_cli.py` — `savings` dispatches against the product's record, not the cwd (the shape
  `RecordCommandUsesTheProductRecordTests:77` already asserts for `groom`).
- `tests/test_tick_steps.py` — `DailyPartsTests` (new, §3.6), beside `DailyStepTests:817`.

**Steps**

1. `cmd_savings` per §2.6: resolve the product and the record through `cli.resolve_record` so the
   first line is `record: <path>`; read the four streams for the window; build `landed_changes` and
   `per_kind`; print the per-kind table, then one line per class — the class, its subject, the
   reading, the threshold and `over` or its distance.
2. Propose every class that is over, ranked by `expected_pct` descending, through
   `record.propose.propose` at the level the caller passed — `rank` on the card is the position × 10,
   so the board sorts them the way the pass does.
3. Re-measure every open card carrying a `savings:` source and write back `measured`, `last_seen`
   and `verdict` (§2.8), then `do_index(root)` **once**, and only when anything was written.
4. The last line: `savings <day>: <n> landed changes, <c> class(es) read, <o> over — <f> filed,
   <b> bumped`.
5. `--dry-run` does steps 1 and 2's reading and printing only: it writes nothing and proposes
   nothing. `--json` prints the readings and the per-kind table as JSON instead of the tables.
6. The daily part, §2.7's entry: `cmd_savings` with `day=yesterday()`, `window=None`, `json=False`,
   `dry_run=False`, the product's name and
   `propose_level=approvals.level_of(product, 'propose_improvement')` — placed immediately before
   `rollup` (PD9). A part that fails is one `daily: savings FAILED — <line>` and the others still
   run (P1).
7. `CommandTests` per §3.6: `asf savings --dry-run` writes nothing; the last line's counts are the
   proposals actually made; `--json` parses. `DailyPartsTests`: `savings` is a part of `daily` and
   comes **before** `rollup`; a failing savings part does not stop the rollup; a card filed by the
   savings part is on the same morning's scorecard — the whole reason §1.4 chose a part over a step.
