§2.2, §2.3's `supersession_findings`, §2.5, §2.6 and §2.7's line text: the whole pure half, in one
new module that does no io, runs no git, reads no product config and imports only `asf.record.core`
and `asf.record.frontmatter` — the same module-level pair `asf/groom/shape.py:9-14` already imports,
which is what makes it importable by `asf.record.check` (Task 2) with no cycle (`asf/groom/__init__.py`
is a docstring and nothing else). Nothing calls it yet: Tasks 2, 5 and 6 are its three callers.

**Files**

- `asf/groom/conflicts.py` — `STATEMENT_HEADINGS`, `CONFLICT_OVERLAP`, `NAMED_OBJECT_RES`,
  `CONFLICT_RE`, `CONFLICT_ANSWER_RE`, `Pair`; `statement_of`, `source_of`, `scope_of`,
  `named_objects`; `supersession_edges`, `cycles`, `is_superseded`, `supersession_findings`;
  `live`, `pair_score`, `pairs`, `precedence`, `decline_key`, `conflict_lines`
- `tests/test_conflicts.py` — `GraphTests`, `ReaderTests`, `PairingTests`, `TopTwentyTests`

**Steps**

1. The module docstring says what the module is and what it may not do — pure, no io, no git, no
   product config, so `asf.record.check` may import it. Every example in it stays clear of
   `tools/<name>.sh` and of a bare `E-nnnn` (PD15).
2. The reader, §2.5: `STATEMENT_HEADINGS`, `statement_of`, `source_of`, `scope_of`,
   `NAMED_OBJECT_RES` and `named_objects`, each exactly as its docstring there says, with the
   one-line comment per regex saying which shape it reads. Sections come from
   `core.parse_sections` — the splitter `expected_body` (`asf/record/core.py:307`) and
   `append_history_lines` already use.
3. The graph, §2.2: `supersession_edges(canonical)` as one de-duplicated `{(loser, winner)}` off
   both fields, so a half-written pair is still one edge; `is_superseded(entry)` true on a
   non-empty `superseded_by` whether the successor exists or not, taking a `rec['meta']` or an
   `index.json` entry (which is the shape Task 3 reads); `cycles(edges)` as the iterative
   three-colour walk over `sorted` nodes, the ring sliced off the stack on a back edge, rotated to
   its lowest id and keyed by that rotation — one finding per ring however it is reached, a
   self-edge a cycle of one.
4. `supersession_findings(canonical, find_line)` (PD6) returning `[(relpath, line, message)]` in
   §2.3's five shapes and no others, each anchored where §2.3 anchors it — the two half-written
   messages on *the other* card's `id:` line, each naming the `asf set` that fixes it. A dangling
   id is reported and then dropped from the edge set before `cycles` runs, so one missing card
   cannot also produce a phantom cycle.
5. The pass, §2.6: `CONFLICT_OVERLAP = 0.5` with its comment (strictly above, as
   `groom_near_duplicates` is strictly above its own, `asf/groom/groom.py:585`); `live`,
   `pair_score`, `pairs`, `precedence` and `decline_key` as their docstrings there specify.
   `precedence` reads `date:`, `stage_since:` then `updated:` off `rec['meta']`, which carries the
   machine block's keys too (PD16). `pairs` is ranked and uncapped (§1.4): the order is the
   guarantee, not a cut.
6. `conflict_lines(canonical)` — one entry per pair, a block whose first line is
   `- [ ] <a> conflicts <b> — <reason>; keep A · keep B · both · merge (merge keeps <winner>) → answer: ____`
   and whose next three lines are indented six spaces: `A <id> "<statement>" — <source>`, the same
   for `B`, `precedence: <winner> (<why>)`. Statements truncated to 120 characters with `…`; the
   reason phrase as §2.7 prints it (`overlap 0.62`, or ``scope `harvest`, shares
   `harvest.rebase_and_resolve` ``).
7. `CONFLICT_RE` and `CONFLICT_ANSWER_RE` as §2.8 spells them but defined here (PD7), each with
   the comment saying who matches it and why the line decides rather than the answer word.
8. `GraphTests` (§3.1), `ReaderTests` (§3.4), `PairingTests` and `TopTwentyTests` (§3.5) — every
   case those four se
