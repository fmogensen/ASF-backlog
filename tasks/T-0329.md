## Description
stories: S-31357
writes: tools/spike/f-0065/**, docs/research/sandboxed-worker-shell.md
after: Task 4

The long one: thirteen probes, `P00`–`P12`, and a `grant-<n>` leg for every one that `bare`
refuses. The coder is the role with the widest reach — it builds, installs, drives a browser, binds
a port and pushes — so it is the role whose table is most likely to say the fence costs more than
it is worth, and the role whose over-granting is the failure the card exists to avoid (§2.5).

**Files**

- `docs/research/sandboxed-worker-shell.md` — §2.7's item 3, first table. §2.6's five columns:
  `probe | needed by the role | under the bare fence | what must be granted | the pair`. The table
  **opens with its two control rows**, both `refused`. `needed by the role` is read off the coder's
  own boundaries in `asf/roles/asf-coder.md`, never guessed.
- `tools/spike/f-0065/**` — PD12 again: an `error` row is fixed and its leg re-run here.

**Steps**

1. `session.sh coder off` and `session.sh coder bare`, under the mode Task 4 proved keeps the fence.
   `off` is the leg that proves the probes themselves work inside a session; a probe that is
   `refused` under `off` is broken, not fenced.
2. Read `bare` and, for each refused probe, add the **one** grant §2.5's slots name for it as its own
   `grant-<n>` leg and launch it. One entry over `bare`, never a bundle: a leg that adds two grants
   cannot say which of them flipped the row.
3. A grant that turns out to be unnecessary is a row too, with the `not needed` verdict (§2.5) — an
   over-granted fence is the thing this card exists to avoid, and the only way that finding gets
   written down is if the leg is launched and read.
4. `P08` deserves its own paragraph in the document whatever it reads (§2.1): the approvals hook
   fails closed, so a refused ledger write can refuse the call that triggered it, and a session
   whose every classified call is refused does nothing and reports nothing. If that is what the
   pair shows, it is a §2.8 row for `asf/hooks.py` and `asf/approvals.py` — and not a Bug minted
   here (PD10, §1.2).
5. `P04`–`P06` reach the real code host read-only with the token the product's
   `conventions.auth_env` already provides. Without it they come back `error`, not `refused`; the
   coder's table is then incomplete in exactly that way and the stamp says so (§4). Do not convert
   an `error` into a verdict (PD12).
6. Generate the table with `collect.py --role coder --markdown` and paste it. Never hand-write a
   row (D12). Update the stamp's session count and unpaired count.
