The spec's third, fourth, fifth and sixth `## In` bullets and `## The design` §§3–4: `gitpush` owns
the words of a killed push and the reader of them, `publish` refuses with the timeout before
anything can bury it, and `republish_steps` counts per branch, caps at two, and writes a park a
person can answer. This is the Task that stops the spend: a branch at the cap is not pushed again,
so the 120 s stops going out every tick.

Read **PD7** (the refs the refusal must name), **PD8** (where the branch goes, and what that
ordering gives up), **PD9** (the routing becomes deterministic), **PD10** (`RUN_FIELDS` is what
makes the clear work), **PD11** (the registry read is never stale) and **PD12** (the harness) before
starting.

   comment, `TIMED_OUT_RE = re.compile(r'push timed out after (\d+)s')` and
   `timed_out_after(text)` returning the limit as an `int` or `None`. `re` joins the imports.
2. `push`'s timeout branch (`:72-73`) keeps computing `refs` from the args and calls
   `line = timeout_line(limit, refs)`. One owner of the words, one reader of them; the stderr it
   appends and the `TIMED_OUT` returncode are unchanged.
3. In `publish`, immediately after `p = gitpush.push(args, wt, timeout=limit)` (`:1668`):

   ```python
       if p.returncode == gitpush.TIMED_OUT:
           return False, f'publish {branch} refused: {gitpush.timeout_line(limit, f"HEAD:{ref}")}'
   ```

   comments byte for byte.
