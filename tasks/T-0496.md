The spec's seventh **In** bullet and `## The design` §`docs/guide/operating.md` and
`docs/guide/getting-started.md`. Both line shapes are already documented prose (P16) and both
sentences are now wrong: `operating.md:27` says a step ends with `[step:<name>] <seconds>s` and
nothing about a start line, and `getting-started.md:143-146` lists the SCHEDULER row's fields without
the step. Last in the wave, because the sentences quote what Tasks 1 and 2 print.

**Files**

- `docs/guide/operating.md` — the sentence at `:27-31` becomes the pair; one new paragraph after the
  `asf watch` sentence at `:35-36`
- `docs/guide/getting-started.md` — the SCHEDULER field list at `:143-146`
- `tests/test_readme.py` — `TickStepLineProseTests`

**Steps**

1. `docs/guide/operating.md`: the sentence beginning "Each step ends with `[step:<name>]
   <seconds>s`" (`:27`) is replaced by the spec's paragraph — each step prints
   `[step:<name>] start owner=<asf|command> pid=<n> at=<time>` as it begins and
   `[step:<name>] <seconds>s ok=<yes|no> owner=… pid=… at=…` as it ends, both flushed at once, so
   `tail -f` names the step the tick is inside right now and not only the ones it has finished; a step
   the tick **skipped** (`off`, already ran today, at CI capacity, already running) prints neither.
   The rest of the paragraph — `tick: state committed and pushed`, `tick: total …`, the two summary
   tables, `[step:<name>] FAILED <why>`, `RECORD STALE`, the `TICK —` digest — is kept byte for byte:
   the `FAILED` line is unchanged by this card (the Out section) and the page must keep saying so.
2. `docs/guide/operating.md` gains, after the `asf watch --product <p>` sentence at `:35-36`, the
   spec's paragraph: `asf doctor` reads that start line too, and the SCHEDULER row for **each clock**
   shows `step: <name> running <age> (<owner>, pid <n>)` while its tick is in a step, so an operator
   can see which step is running without opening the log. Per clock, because each clock has its own
   log (P19) — the sentence says so, since a product with three clocks gets three answers.
3. `docs/guide/getting-started.md`: the SCHEDULER field list at `:143-146` gains `step:` in the
   parenthesised list — the step that job's tick is inside right now, with its age, owner and pid —
   and the sentence after the RED one: a job whose last tick was killed inside a step is `YELLOW`,
   naming the step it did not finish. The existing RED sentences (non-zero exit, never ran in two
   intervals, no loaded job at all) and the `NEEDS OPERATOR: step <s> is on no clock` sentence are
   kept byte for byte.
4. What neither page may claim: that a skipped step prints a start line; that the field is present on
   every row (it is absent whenever the clock's log has no per-step line in its last 1 MiB, which
   includes every log written before this change); that YELLOW fails an install (it does not — it is
   never the doctor's exit code, P9); or that anything reads a state file. The `step:` field is
   described as read off the log, which is what D8 chose.
5. `TickStepLineProseTests` in `tests/test_readme.py`, in the shape `ProductResolutionProseTests`
   (`:399-421`) already uses — read back from the checkout through `REPO_ROOT` (`:14`), not asserted
   against a copy: `operating.md` names `[step:<name>] start owner=`, `ok=<yes|no>`, `pid=`, `at=`,
   the word "flushed" and each of the four skip reasons, and **no longer** carries the old sentence
   "Each step ends with `[step:<name>] <seconds>s`"; `operating.md` names `asf doctor` and the
   `step: <name> running` field; `getting-started.md`'s SCHEDULER paragraph names `step:` and carries
   a `YELLOW` sentence naming a tick killed inside a step; and both pages still name
   `[step:<name>] FAILED`, `last-exit` and `last-run`, so the fields this card does not touch cannot
   be dropped by accident.
