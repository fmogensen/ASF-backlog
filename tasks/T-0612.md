The spec's §4: one string. It moves this card's own number by nothing — `kind:review`'s share is
review spend ÷ **all** session spend (P13), and `is_repair` gates only which kinds may become a
*cause*, neither the numerator nor the denominator — which is exactly what makes it safe to land
inside this card's own measurement window. What it changes is that when F-0060 lands and spend moves
out of `review-…` jobs into `precheck-…` jobs, that spend stays visible as its own repair cause
instead of falling into `other` and reading as a saving nobody made (D6).

Read **PD14** (no collision, and the boundary this Task does not cross) before starting.

**Files**

- `asf/scorecard/score.py` — `'precheck'` in `REPAIR_PREFIXES` (`:24-26`).
- `tests/test_scorecard.py` — **new class** `RepairPrefixTests`.

**Steps**

1. `asf/scorecard/score.py`: add `'precheck'` to `REPAIR_PREFIXES`, in the spec's position — after
   `'prereview'`, before `'rebase'`. Nothing else in the module changes: `session_kind`,
   `is_repair`, `is_correction`, `DEAD_PREFIXES`, `CORRECTION_PREFIXES` and `_JOB_RE` are untouched.
2. Nothing in `asf/scorecard/diagnose.py` changes: no key is renamed, no cause is regrouped, no
   threshold moves, `causes` and `metric` read exactly as they do now.
3. `metrics.KINDS` and its `KIND_PREFIXES` (`asf/metrics/metrics.py:42-46`) are **not** touched —
   the spec's `## Out` gives that line to F-0060's Task 6 (PD14).
4. `tests/test_scorecard.py` `RepairPrefixTests`: `score.is_repair('precheck')` is True; a session
   whose job name begins `precheck-` reads kind `precheck` through `session_kind`'s job-name rule
   (`precheck-t-0271` → `precheck`); a `kind:precheck` cause can cross `kind_share` and be filed
   (`diagnose.causes`' gate at `:287-292` reads `c['repair']`, which is `score.is_repair(k)`); and —
   the point of the Story — `diagnose.metric(facts, 'kind:review', …)` returns **the same number**
   over a fixture containing precheck sessions before and after the change. Write that last one as
   a direct comparison: patch `score.REPAIR_PREFIXES` back to the tuple without `'precheck'` for one
   call and assert the two readings are equal, so the assertion is over the change itself and not
   over a number someone typed.
