The spec's `## The design` §4 and §5, against the reader that ships today. After this lands, a job
whose `runs-on` this reader cannot read stops being invisible: `asf doctor` carries one advisory row
per such job naming the workflow, the job and the raw expression, `asf ci reconcile` prints one line
per job after its table, and **no step of the plan removes any label while one stands** — the plan's
adds still go, a label a `ci.reserve` owns still follows `reserve_plan`, and every other candidate
removal moves to a new `Step.held` list whose action clause says why it waits. On a product with a
`${{ matrix.os }}` job this does useful work at once; its real purpose is that Task 2 is then safe to
land (**PD1**).

**Nothing resolves any new expression yet.** `_EXPR_DEFAULT` still guesses `heavy` out of
`${{ vars.X || 'heavy' }}`, `runs_on()` reads no variables, and `drift` is untouched. Tasks 2 and 3
are what change that.

Read **PD2** before starting: every piece of this Task works against today's `labels is None`, and
the four fields it adds (`RunsOn.event`, `RunsOn.expr`, `Step.held`) are defaulted fields on
dataclasses built positionally everywhere and with keywords nowhere. Read **PD7** before writing
`expr`: the spec's sketch is `expr='' if labels else raw`, and for `runs-on: []` that disagrees with
`unresolved`. Read **PD10** for the row order and the two early returns of `doctor_rows` that must
stay exactly as they are.
