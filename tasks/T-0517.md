The spec's first **In** bullet's remaining half and `## The design` §`asf/workers/retention.py` — the
settled rule. The chain is PD1's, not the spec's `elif` (read PD1 before writing a line of this).
Everything the two older rules do stays as it is, including every line they print.

**Files**

- `asf/workers/retention.py` — the module docstring's rule list (`:3-11`); `candidates` (`:168-203`)
  — signature, the rule chain, `due`'s fifth field; `sweep`'s unpacking site, `what`, and
  `write_state`'s `reaped`; `REAPED_KEPT`
- `tests/test_retention.py` — `DUE` and three `RetentionSweep` assertions; a new `SettledRule` class

**Steps**

1. `REAPED_KEPT = 200` beside `DAY_S` (`:43`), with a `#:` comment naming what it caps and why
   (D5: a delete under `settled` removes a ref the forge may have been the only holder of, and the
   sha is what makes it restorable).
2. The module docstring (`:3-11`): "Two rules" becomes three, and the new rule gets a paragraph in
   the same shape as `archive` and `legacy` — its name, what it covers, and that the age is the
   tip's commit date (PD17), not the day the card closed.
3. `candidates` takes `items` and `contained` as its last two parameters, in the spec's order, and
   its docstring's `due` shape becomes `[(branch, sha, rule, age_days, why)]`.
4. The rule chain: **PD1's, byte for byte** — the legacy arm carries its own "a prefix the product
   still mints is never legacy" predicate, and the settled arm is tried on every head the first two
   did not take, once, its result held in `why`. `settled_s = conv.retention('settled_days') * DAY_S`
   with the spec's `# 0: the rule is off (D8)` comment; `if rule is None and settled_s` is what that
   zero turns off. `why` is `''` for the two older rules and never None.
5. Everything below the chain is untouched and stays in this order: the trunk / release / protected
   refusal, the moved-tip check, the age check, then the three holds. Under the new chain `main`,
   `release/1.0` and a protected head all now *reach* a rule arm and are refused by the line below
   it — that is intended, and step 9's cases are the net under it. `due.append` gains `why`;
   `due.sort` and `result['due'].extend` are unchanged (PD14).
6. `sweep`: pass `items` and `contained` to `candidates`. The unpacking site becomes
   `for b, sha, rule, days, why in due[:cap]`, and `what` gains the reason only when there is one —
   `if why: what += f' — {why}'` — so the two older rules' lines stay byte-identical and a settled
   delete reads `retention: deleted legacy/T-0009 (settled, 30d old) — T-0009 is Closed in the
   record`. The dry-run line takes the same suffix:
   `retention: would delete integration/backfill-spec (settled, 22d old) — the trunk carries it`.
7. `reaped`: for every head this pass actually deleted, `{'branch': b, 'sha': sha, 'rule': rule,
   'why': why, 'at': stamp}` — the same iso stamp the state file carries. `write_state`'s payload
   gains `'reaped': (previous['reaped'] + this pass's)[-REAPED_KEPT:]`, read off `read_state` and
   tolerant of a file that has no `reaped` key or whose value is not a list, newest last (D5). A
   delete that failed is not in it.
8. `tests/test_retention.py`: `DUE` becomes
   `['archive/cloud/old', 'docs/closed', 'hb/dead', 'legacy/T-0009']`, and the three `RetentionSweep`
   assertions in PD3's Task 3 half take their new values — the `legacy_prefixes: ['hb/']` due list,
   and `per_tick: 1` deleting `legacy/T-0009` first (its 30-day tip is the oldest due), leaving three
   and printing `3 more expired, left for the next pass`, with the second pass deleting
   `archive/cloud/old`.
