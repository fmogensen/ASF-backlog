The spec's `unretire` bullet and `## The design` §5. P7 is the reported defect in one line:
`ingest_into` is `if type_ not in EVIDENCE_TYPES or (type_ == 'feature' and is_retired(rec['meta'])):
continue` (`asf/record/ingest.py:1101`), and `is_retired` is `bool(meta.get('removed') or
meta.get('moved_to'))` — so a Feature the pre-ASF migration marked `removed: history…` gets no state,
no stage, no `evidence:` line and no History line at all. Shipped work is not mis-counted; it is
invisible.

It waits on Task 3 because the evidence that un-retires a Feature **is** its children's typed
answer: D9 un-retires on the Feature's own landing *or* at least one child's, and Task 3 is what puts
a child's landing on the record. It also waits because both Tasks write `asf/backfill/apply.py` and
`tests/test_backfill.py`.

`removed:` is **cleared**, not annotated (§5): anything short of clearing it leaves every
`is_retired` reader still skipping the card, which is the whole defect. Read **PD9** (there is a
second retired guard at `:867`, and clearing the field answers both) before starting.

**Files**

- `asf/backfill/apply.py` — `unretire(root, product, items, found, out=print)`, beside
  `type_landings`
- `tests/test_backfill.py` — `UnretireRule`

**Steps**

1. `unretire(root, product, items, found, out=print)` with the spec's §5 docstring byte for byte.
   Its gate is D9, and it is two conditions, both required: the Feature's `removed:` text is a
   **history marker**, and backfill proved a landing for the Feature's own `legacy_id` or for at
   least one of its children.
2. The history-marker test is a predicate over the `removed:` text, and it is deliberately narrow:
   a `removed:` an operator wrote for any other reason is **not** a history marker and the Feature
   stays retired. Put the predicate in `apply.py` as a module-level constant with its reasoning in
   the docstring — never a product's own wording, which P16's fence forbids in `asf/**/*.py` anyway.
   A `removed:` text the predicate does not recognise is a residue row, not a write.
3. A card carrying `moved_to:` is **never touched**, whatever its `removed:` says: it was not hidden
   by history, it was moved, and `is_retired` reads both keys. Test it.
4. The children searched are the Feature's descendants as the record holds them, so a landing on a
   Task two levels down counts. Reuse `match._descendants(items, fid)` (`asf/record/match.py:98-105`)
   rather than walking `children` by hand — the same traversal rule 4 uses when it scopes a
   `<slug>-t<n>` lookup, so "a child" means the same thing in both halves of this card.
5. The write, through `setfield.set_typed` under the `backfill` writer: `removed: None`,
   `unretired: <date>`, and one `## History` line — `- <date> backfill: unretired — <n> child
   landing(s), newest <sha> (<date>)`. `unretired:` is the durable marker in the shape `reopen`'s
   `reopened:` established: a key `ingest` does not derive and therefore never drops (invariant I1),
   so the reversal is on the record forever and a second run sees it and does nothing.
6. Return the ids un-retired. A Feature with no landing found for it or any child keeps its
   `removed:` **byte-identically** and is returned as a residue row for Task 6's table — assert the
   bytes, not just the field.
7. No state, no stage, no `evidence:` line is written here either (D1). The derived state arrives the
   ordinary way, with dates: the children carry `landed: <sha>` from Task 3, `ingest` derives
   `children-closed` or `children-resolved`, and the Feature's `evidence:` says which rule and its
   History says when. That is the card's *"turn `removed: history…` Features into their derived landed
   state with dates instead of hiding them"* — done by deriving, not by asserting.
