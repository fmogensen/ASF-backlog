§2.4: one clause in `load_rules`, beside the retirement clause at `asf/rules/rules.py:96-97`. Because
`superseded_by:` is typed it reaches the index entry with no schema change, so this Task needs
nothing from any other — it reads a key off `index.json` and skips the row.

**Files**

- `asf/rules/rules.py` — the `superseded_by` clause in `load_rules`, and its docstring naming it
- `tests/test_rules.py` — `SupersededRuleTests`

**Steps**

1. In `load_rules`, immediately after `if entry.get('removed') or entry.get('moved_to'): continue`:
   `if entry.get('superseded_by'): continue  # replaced by another rule: its successor binds the
   record in its place`. The comment is the reason, not the restatement — §1.4's choice is that a
   superseded rule is *skipped*, never counted as an unenforced gap, because its successor is
   enforced in its place.
2. The docstring's one sentence gains the second retirement: a card carrying `removed:`,
   `moved_to:` **or** `superseded_by:` is not live.
3. Nothing else moves: `partition`, `run_all` and the `== RULES <n> checked, <v> violations, <u>
   unenforced` line all count what `load_rules` returned, so the rule leaves all three counts at
   once and its check script is never resolved or run.
4. `SupersededRuleTests` per §3.3, on `make_repo()` with `write_rule`/`write_check` and the
   module's own `reindex()` (PD17 — a scalar `superseded_by: R-0001` needs no change to its
   mini-parser): `R-0001` live with a check that exits 1 on a violation line, `R-0002` the same
   plus `superseded_by: R-0001`. `rules.load_rules(root)` returns `['R-0001']`; `rules check` exits
   1; its first line is `== RULES 1 checked, 1 violations, 0 unenforced`; `R-0002`'s violation line
   is absent from stdout and the successor's is present.
5. The script is never executed: `R-0002`'s check writes a marker file, and the file does not exist
   after the run. This is the assertion that says the skip happens at load, not at print.
6. The three remaining cases of §3.3: with `superseded_by` removed the same record reads
   `== RULES 2 checked, 2 violations, 0 unenforced`; a rule carrying `superseded_by: R-0099`, a
   successor that does not exist, is still skipped; and a superseded rule with `enforced: false`
   appears in no unenforced list and `--verbose` says nothing about it.
