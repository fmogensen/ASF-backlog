## Description
stories: S-37350, S-37357, S-37358
writes: docs/products.example.yaml, docs/guide/ci-runner-pool.md
after: Task 3, Task 4, Task 6

The spec's §2.7, and nothing else. After this lands, an operator can read how to stop maintaining a
runner list, what the two tiers mean, why a box does not flap between them, why the fast tier is never
empty, and how a product's own timing guard asks ASF what this box should take on this job.

This Task is last because everything it documents must already be true. It is also the one Task whose
footprint is fenced by tests it does not own: `tests/test_env.py:37` and
`tests/test_approvals.py:283-285` both assert `env.validate_product_text(docs/products.example.yaml)
== []`, and `tests/test_readme.py` reads four guide files. **Read PD19:** the example declares all
three `capacity.batch` keys, so D16's measured path is one the fenced document does not take, and its
`ci.pool` declares roles `heavy` and `light`, outside the namespace Task 4 now refuses. The discovered
form goes in as a **commented** alternative beside the declared list, so the example keeps exercising
the declared path that every other test depends on.

**Files**

- `docs/products.example.yaml` — three comment blocks and no new uncommented key. The `ci.pool`
  block (`:225-235`) gains the discovered form and what it implies: `pool: discover` and the list are
  the two forms; discovery reads the host's registered runners into a census the tick refreshes; the
  tier (`asf-fast`, `asf-bulk`) is placed from measured speed and never declared, so a `role` or a
  `class` in `asf-` is refused; `box`, `provider` and `size` are not reported by the host and are
  empty on a discovered entry, so `ci.reserve`'s `spread_by: box` has no effect (O2, PD10); and the
  slot sum is still this product's CI ceiling unless `capacity.ci` sets one (P4). The `ci.reserve`
  documentation loses `label` from its required keys and gains the default — `asf-pr-<of>` with a
  leading `asf-` stripped from `of`, so `of: asf-fast` gives `asf-pr-fast` — and says a hand-written
  label in `asf-` is refused while one outside it is still accepted. `capacity.batch` (`:244-247`)
  gains one line saying `parallel` and `runners` are measured from the census and the run's expected
  jobs when the product declares **neither**, that declaring either leaves both alone (D16), and that
  `per_run` is never measured (O4). Nothing in `ci.labels` changes (PD18).
- `docs/guide/ci-runner-pool.md` — two sections. **The census and the tier:** `ci.pool: discover`;
  what a census is and where it lives; the two tiers and that a tier *is* a runner's routing label,
  so every reader that understood a role understands a tier; the band between `PROMOTE_AT` and
  `DEMOTE_AT` and that it is what stops a box on the boundary changing tier every tick (D6); the
  provisional tier a box with fewer than `MIN_READINGS` green readings carries and why it is the
  bulk one (D7); the floor that promotes the best box whatever its ratio rather than let the fast
  tier empty (D8); that a promotion is judged by one job and rolled back with a Bug if that job is
  red (D10); `asf ci census` and `--apply`; and the doctor rows that name a slow box and a flaky one.
  **Reading a baseline from a product's own guard:** `asf ci baseline --job <kind> --runner <name>`
  prints one number; `--json` says whether it came from the runner or its tier; and a job kind with
  a reading on neither **exits 1 printing nothing**, because a guard that cannot get a baseline
  should skip its check rather than invent one. Say plainly what O1 refuses and why: ASF does not
  read a box's cores, memory or disk, because it measures the only thing every consumer wants — how
  long this box takes on this job kind — and inspecting hardware would need a login to every CI box
  that this product does not have and has not asked for.
