§2.6 — the card's "listed for the groom", and the record edit that answers it. One section, one
answer word, and a refusal that keeps the containment law true rather than assuming it.

**Files**

- `asf/groom/groom.py` — `GROOM_SECTIONS` (`:464`) gains
  `('Branches held at the writes boundary', 'held_boundary')` last but one, before
  `Auto-filed Bugs not yet decided`; `build_groom_sections` (`:505`) gains `held_boundary=()` and
  renders it; `cmd_groom` (`:670`) fills it from `lifecycle.corrections(pool.sessions_path(product))`
  kept to `kind == 'boundary'`, joined to each card's grant; `ANSWER_GRANT` and `_parse_answer`
  (`:58`); the applier's `grant` arm beside the other typed fields in `apply_groom_answers`.
- `tests/test_groom.py` — `BoundarySectionTests` (new); `AnswerParsingTests:102` gains the two
  `grant` cases; `GroomSectionCoverageTests:293` needs no edit and is what fails if the section is
  added to `GROOM_SECTIONS` and not to the builder.

**Steps**

1. The section line, in the shape `_LINE_ID_RE` already reads (the id first, so `_line_sections`
   attributes an answer to it):
   `- [ ] T-0080 outside its grant on worker/T-0080: <paths> — grant: <globs> → answer: ____`.
   The paths come from the correction's `outside` key (Task 3 put them there); a correction written
   before that key existed falls back to the paths its text names.
2. `build_groom_sections` stays a pure function of its inputs: `held_boundary` is a list of
   `(item, branch, outside, grant)`. No product and no ledger → an empty section rendering `(none)`,
   as every other section with nothing to say does.
3. `cmd_groom` fills it where it already reads the ledger (P10), beside `_ledger_items`.
4. `ANSWER_GRANT = re.compile(r'^grant\s+(\S+)$', re.IGNORECASE)` → `('grant', '<glob>')` from
   `_parse_answer`, after the existing words.
5. The applier's arm: append the glob to the card's `grant:` (creating the field when absent),
   through `_write_card`, with the history line `grant: + <glob>` and the usual attribution. A glob
   already there is a no-op, which is what keeps `--apply` idempotent.
6. The refusal: before writing, `boundary.within([glob], card['writes'])` — a glob that leaves the
   Task's footprint is not written; the line is left unanswered with `(refused: outside its
   writes:)` appended. `no` on such a line stays the existing `removed:` answer; every other word is
   unrecognised, as today.
7. `BoundarySectionTests`: one line per held branch with item, branch, outside paths and grant;
   `(none)` with none; the applier appending to an absent and to an existing `grant:` with the
   history line; the refused glob leaving the line unanswered and the card unchanged.
