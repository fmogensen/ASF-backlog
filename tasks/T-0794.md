The spec's §*The design* §1 and §2 in full: `released_since`, `adjudications` reading it beside
`ruled_since`, `stalemate_release`, and `cmd_unpark`'s third source.

This is the half that pays first. Nothing the factory *decides* changes until a release exists: with
no `unparked` stamp anywhere, `released_since` returns `''`, the `or` short-circuits to today's
answer, and every row reads as it reads now.

Read **PD2** (the reader is `lifecycle.runs` filtered on the item, and why `item_runs` makes the new
test unwriteable), **PD4** (what `stalemate_release` deliberately does not check) and **PD12** (the
guard stays, and the two-parks case passes without it) before starting.

**Files**

- `asf/tick/step_wave.py` — beside `ruled_since` (**:443**), in its shape and with the spec's
  docstring:
  - `released_since(product, item, at, path=None)` — `_stamp(at)`, `''` when that is empty; then
    the item-wide `max` of `_stamp(r.get('unparked'))` over the runs of `item`, keeping only
    stamps `>= since`, `default=''`. The runs come from
    `lifecycle.runs(path or pool_mod.sessions_path(product))` filtered on `r.get('item') == item`
    (**PD2** — not `lifecycle.item_runs`). The docstring says what the field means and why it is
    read item-wide, citing `lifecycle.same_head_loop` (**:2856**, its own `max` at **:2874**).
  - `adjudications` (**:368**) — the ruling test at **:406-407** becomes the ruling **or** the
    release, under the same `if cur['same_card']` guard and with no other change:
    `if cur['same_card'] and (ruled_since(product, item, cur['at'])
    or released_since(product, item, cur['at'])): cur['same_card'] = False`. Order is for cost
    only: `ruled_since` reads the card off disk, `released_since` reads the fold the function is
    already walking, and `or` short-circuits either way. The `if cur['same_card']:` block at
    **:408** is **not** touched — a released item therefore carries no `ruling` and no `carried`
    key, which is right: it is going back to adjudication, not past it.
- `asf/workers/unpark.py` — one helper and one branch:
  - `stalemate_release(path, item)` — `(job, run)` of the item's newest **ended** adjudicate run
    (`r.get('item') == item and r.get('kind') == 'adjudicate' and r.get('ended')`), keyed on
    `r.get('started') or ''`, over `lifecycle.runs(path)`; `(None, None)` when there is none. The
    docstring names `rows._capped` as what the park is derived from. **PD4**: no attempts test, no
    `same_card` test.
  - `cmd_unpark` (**:82**) — `sjob, srun = stalemate_release(path, item) if scope ==
    lifecycle.SCOPE_ITEM else (None, None)`, computed after `by_factory` (**:108**) and before the
    refusal (**:109**), which becomes `if not by_hand and not by_factory and not sjob:`. Then,
    after the factory loop (**:119-121**) and before `if why:` (**:122**), the third release:
    `if sjob and not by_hand and not by_factory:` → `pool_mod.update_session(product, sjob,
    unparked=now, unpark_why=why)` and one printed line naming the job, the run's stamp and what
    happens next. **No `correction=None`** — there is no correction to clear, and writing one
    would retire a pending correction the park never owned. **Item scope only** — a branch or a
    job target names a branch or a job, and the derived park holds the item.
