The spec's §3 and §5's first half. `waiting()` has parked every product *but* the marker's owner
since B-0141, by design and for a stated reason (P4) — and that exemption is exactly why no gap
ever came: the one product that must find an empty floor is the one product that keeps putting
processes on it. From `DUE_GRACE_S` after a debt is recorded, every tick of every product parks,
the owner included, until the floor is empty or the stretch caps out. And while a debt is recorded,
no tick spawns a background harvest — so the floor can only shrink.

**Read PD4 before writing `draining()`**: the stretch is derived from `at`, never carried in a
mutable `parked_at`, because `draining` and `bump_due` are two read-modify-write writers of one
file and a lost update turns a bounded park into an unbounded one.

**What changes for the machine when this lands.** A floor that will not empty by itself now empties
on purpose. For the first five minutes of a debt nothing changes — the start drain and Task 2's
exit attempts get a full clock interval with nobody parked (C4's grace). After that every tick of
every product prints `tick: waiting — upgrade to 5cab3a2 due since 06:25 — pass 4 (owner asf); the
floor is draining` and runs no step; a parked tick exits in a second and spawns no gate, so the
floor shrinks until the last process out installs. Ten minutes of that and the park lifts with one
`NEEDS OPERATOR` line and the factory runs for ten minutes before another stretch — the debt stays
on disk through all of it and is still attempted at every exit. B-0141's rule is intact: a stuck
upgrade never stops the factory.
