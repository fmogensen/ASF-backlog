after: Task 4

`release_report` step 9, §`asf/tick/file_bugs.py`, D10, D11 — and PD4, which is where this Task
does not do what the spec's step 9 literally says.

**Files**

- `asf/upgrade.py` — step 9, replacing Task 4's window
- `asf/tick/file_bugs.py` — `UPGRADE_RED_SIG`, `file_upgrade_bug`
- `tests/test_release_channel.py` — `RedDoctorRollsBackTests`
- `tests/test_file_bugs.py` — `UpgradeRedBugTests`

**Steps**

1. Step 9 opens by printing `rollback: doctor RED after <tag> — reinstalling <old>`. The wording is
   the spec's and it matters twice: `release._ROLLBACK_RE` is `\brolled back\b|\brollback:`
   (`asf/release.py:53`), so this line lands in the upgrade-safety criterion as a rollback and
   holds the next release gate — which is the correct consequence, not a side effect.
2. `_install(old, run, out, sha=old_sha)` — **the previous tag, verified by the previous sha**
   (PD4). The spec writes `_install(<old sha>, …)`; installing by sha makes pipx record a sha as
   `requested_revision`, `cli._release` then returns `None`, and D6 holds the product on that pin
   for good — the fixed `v0.1.64` could never be taken and a human would have to rerun the
   installer, which is what this card exists to stop needing. Installing the tag with the sha as
   the verification target gets the identical package and a readable pin. `old` is never `None`
   here: step 3's `newer(tag, old)` is False whenever it is, so the `auto` path cannot reach step 9
   without a readable old tag, and the spec's `or old` fallback is the dead half of its expression.
3. It is `_install` and not `install(owner=…)` (D10): the marker/drain protocol has already run and
   cleared, and re-entering it would write a second pending marker — for a *downgrade* — and park
   every product's ticks for it.
4. Then `_new_asf(['scheduler', 'install', '--product', product.name], run)` on the restored
   binary: the clocks must be the old package's again, and `venv_bin` now resolves to it.
5. `file_bugs.UPGRADE_RED_SIG = 'upgrade {tag}: doctor RED'` and
   `file_upgrade_bug(root, tag, previous, evidence, default_bug_epic=None, out=print)`, reusing
   `_file_or_bump_bug` unchanged — only the signature and the `info` dict are new (D11). `info`
   carries `title` (`asf <tag> fails its doctor — the product is held at <previous>`),
   `severity: 'S1'`, `evidence` (the doctor's own RED rows, one per line), `runs: []` and
   `acceptance`. Both `runs` and `evidence` are indexed and not `.get` in the existing writer
   (`asf/tick/file_bugs.py:339,348`), so both must be present (PD10). `S1` sets `decided: True` for
   free (`:360`, B-0089): a red release does not wait for the daily groom. One Bug per
   `(release tag, doctor RED)`, bumped once a day — the same bad release hits every product on the
   channel and every tick until somebody acts, and three cards for one fact is the thing D11 refuses.
6. The evidence is the doctor's RED rows and nothing else: `_new_asf`'s captured output, filtered to
   the lines whose status column is `RED` (`doctor.format_table`'s shape,
   `asf/doctor.py:1055-1067`), capped. The whole doctor table in a Bug body is noise; the rows that
   are red are the finding.
7. The Bug is committed and pushed by the channel itself — `shadow.commit_local(root, message)` then
   `shadow.push(root)` on `ctx.record_root()` (P12) — because the tick ends before its record step
   and a Bug written and never pushed is a Bug nobody reads. Both calls are inside the channel's own
   `try`: a push that fails costs the line on stderr and nothing else, and the next RED tick bumps
   the same signature.
8. Record `last` with `result: 'rolled-back'` (`from`, `from_sha`, `to`, `at`), which is what Task
   6's doctor RED case reads, and return `'rolled-back'`. The tick then ends on the fork Task 3
   landed.
9. The *record* is not rolled back, only the package
