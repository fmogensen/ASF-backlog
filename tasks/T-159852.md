The spec's design §6 whole, and the card's outcome 4. `asf.capacity` already answers the
multi-product question — the operator's machine total less the sessions in flight elsewhere, this
product's own ceiling, its fair share of the usable pool by weight and the quota band under it
(`P7`, `P8`) — and the kernel resolves its own number instead, over **one product's** session
ledger, and launches by calling `spawn.spawn` directly, so the host-wide seat claim
`asf.workers.pool.Pool.take` takes under a `flock` is never taken. Two kernels on one host each read
an account as free and each fill it to its cap (`P6`).

**This is the one Task in the plan whose fenced source patch is wrong in a way that would be a
release-sized regression, and `PC1` is the row that says so.** Read `PC1` and `PC2` before writing a
line of `asf/kernel/ports.py`.

**Read this Task from `docs/plans/f-0334.md`, not from this card.** The card's prose is cut at 4000
characters (`PC8`). Three decision rows bind it and are on no card: **`PC1`** (§6's fenced
`capacity()` resolves to `4` on the factory's own product shape — measured — because
`capacity.DEFAULT_SESSIONS` is `4` and `Resolved.sessions` is never `None`; keep today's body and
bound it only when `r.sessions_bound != 'default'`), **`PC2`** (`asf.workers.seats` has no `take`
and no `cap=`; and `Pool.take` skips the cap check entirely for a cloud lane, so the kernel must
