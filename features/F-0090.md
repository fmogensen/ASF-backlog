## Description

Measured 2026-09-23: **32 adjudicate runs, 23.7 hours, all on Opus** — the most expensive model in
the pool, spending a fifth of all session hours on the factory arguing with itself. Opus totals
88 runs and 69.4 hours; adjudication is its single largest use.

What the adjudications actually decided, read back: T-0026's ruling was "Task 8353 must land
first" — a dependency, now one `after:` line. Two more were launched this morning for T-0026 and
T-0027 *after* that line was on the cards, and had to be killed by hand. An item whose predecessor
has not landed is not in dispute; it is waiting. An item whose brief is stale is not in dispute;
it is misinformed.

Adjudication is for a genuine disagreement about *this* item's code — two rounds that changed the
same file in incompatible ways. Everything else has a cheaper answer, and the cheaper answer has
to be tried first.

## Acceptance

- Before an adjudicate row is produced, the row is tested against the cheaper causes in order:
  an unlanded `after:` predecessor (hold), a brief older than the item's last change (rebuild the
  brief and re-run the same kind), a gate red for a reason outside the item's `writes:` (hold with
  that reason). Only what survives all three is a dispute.
- An adjudication states its ruling as typed fields the record can act on — `blocked_on`,
  `writes` correction, `superseded_by` — and its prose is the explanation, never the mechanism.
- The tick prints why an adjudicate row was raised, in one line, naming the two rounds it is
  between.
- Target, measured in a week: adjudicate runs under 10 % of Opus hours.
