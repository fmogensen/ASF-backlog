## Statement
A workhorse-model mechanical pre-review runs before every deep-model spec review
(the pre-review brief template, nine checks, verdict `CLEAN` or
`BOUNCE` back to the writer); deep-model reviews only judgment. The five rules adopted with it, all
binding:

1. A brief names its template by path (the plans directory… ## Wave 0) and pre-lists the
 files the spec will cite — header the spec-writer brief header.
2. Model routing: deep-model writes specs, runs round-1 reviews and preflights; workhorse-model applies a
 preflight's rulings to a plan, does the revision of a revision, every re-check, and codes.
3. Probe before any relaunch, and probe every in-flight record at the hourly check-in
 (a per-account probe record); relaunch only on evidence of death.
4. Before pushing a train head, run the touched packages' vitest suites locally — ten minutes beats
 a 1.5 h CI round; consumer tests of a reworded string are the usual miss.
5. Batch status into the hourly check-in unless something needs the operator.

## Why
On 2026-09-15 three specs (P5-A, P5-B, P3-B) each cost a deep-model review, a deep-model revision and a
workhorse-model re-check — roughly 15 to 20 points per spec — and nearly every round-1 CHANGES REQUESTED was
the same mechanical classes: empty `it()` bodies, cited exports that do not exist, tests written
against a harness that does not exist, pre-rename names. Writers spent their first twenty minutes
grepping for files the brief could have named, every brief pasted a 3,000-token template, two dead
planning-model sessions produced two duplicate relaunches and nothing else, and a red train cost a
controller diagnosis plus a CI round.

## Check
None yet. The source check for this rule sources a shared library bound to one product's repository, state directory, branch naming and scheduled job, so it cannot be carried as it stands. It becomes a check when the rule runner reads those four facts from the product's configuration.
