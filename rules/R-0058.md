## Statement
A launch goes to the account with the most 5-hour and 7-day room that is not already this branch's writer, reviewer or fixer. Every spawn is routed by the picker; hand-picking is only ever for the exclusion argument, never for the choice. Big jobs are staggered so the windows do not reset together — a 5-hour window starts at the account's first request after its reset, so the offsets hold as long as each account keeps getting work, and they are re-set after any pause that lets two accounts fall idle together. A full plan review or a heavy revision counts double when two land on one account inside one window.

## Why
Routing by habit fills one account and leaves three idle. On 2026-09-14 two accounts hit 100 % of their 5-hour windows within two minutes of each other after about six hours of two concurrent heavy jobs each, cutting off a review with no output and a plan revision with 740 of 788 lines uncommitted in its worktree. On 2026-09-15 the same thing happened again, by hand-picking: three consecutive heavy reviews went to one account inside one window because the picker was never consulted. A cut-off job leaves its worktree — it is never cleaned by name until its work is committed.

## Check
None yet. The source check for this rule sources a shared library bound to one product's repository, state directory, branch naming and scheduled job, so it cannot be carried as it stands. It becomes a check when the rule runner reads those four facts from the product's configuration.
