## Description

`conventions.harvest.gate_timeout_s` was set to 300 on 2026-09-22, when the sharded suite ran in
about 60 seconds — "five times that is a hang". The suite is now **1371 tests, ~95 seconds on an
idle machine**, and the gate shares that machine with up to eight worker sessions each running
their own suite. Under that load the gate exceeds 300 seconds on perfectly green work.

What a timed-out gate costs: the branch is held, its round counter climbs, and at the cap the
item is handed to an **Opus adjudication** — for a clock, not a defect. Today it happened to
`worker/T-0042` ("adjudicate pending") and `worker/T-0047` (round 3), and both are the cards that
have been sitting on the floor all morning.

The timeout is now 1800 s in the operator's product file, which stops the bleeding. The defect is
that the number was a guess against a measurement that has since moved by a factor of six, and
nothing noticed.

## Acceptance

- The gate timeout is derived, not guessed: the product records the gate's own measured duration
  (the harvest already times it) and the timeout is a multiple of the recent maximum, with a floor
  and a ceiling from config.
- A gate that times out is never counted as a round and never advances an item toward
  adjudication: the tick prints `gate timed out` as its own outcome, and the branch is retried
  next tick.
- `asf doctor` reports the gate's measured duration beside its timeout, so a drift of this size
  is visible before it holds a branch.
- A test: a gate whose command exceeds the timeout leaves `rounds` unchanged.
