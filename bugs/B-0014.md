## Description
`tools/cutover.sh <product> --apply --force` retired the pre-`asf` tool directories while `asf tick` carried only step 0 (the record): the dispatch half (health, launch wave, PR opener, batches, the daily job) had no replacement, and launches stopped for three minutes until the directories were restored by hand. The kit also rewrote the scheduler's plist with a bare `python3`, no working directory and no `PYTHONPATH`, so the job could not have started without a hand fix. Installing the product must leave a working factory or refuse.

## Fix
1. `cutover.sh` refuses (no `--force` override) to retire a directory that a loaded scheduler job still references; it lists the job and the path.
2. `cutover.sh` refuses when `asf tick` does not yet carry every step the scheduler ran before (a manifest of steps: record, health, wave, prs, batch, daily; each either owned by `asf` or still owned by a listed legacy job).
3. The scheduler adapter writes a plist/cron line with the interpreter's absolute path, the package's working directory, `PATH`/`HOME`/`PYTHONPATH`, and a log file; `asf doctor` prints the loaded factory jobs with their last exit and last run, and fails on a job that has never completed.
4. Acceptance: on a machine with the legacy jobs loaded, `cutover.sh --apply` either produces a factory whose next scheduled run commits `tick: state` to the record's origin and launches a wave, or exits non-zero having changed nothing.
