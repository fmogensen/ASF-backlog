## Statement
The repository grant is per account, not per machine, and every account carries it; a routine created without it fails with a permission error. A person does the grant; the controller verifies it with a disabled probe routine that never fires.

Two mechanics bind every dispatch. The seat label does not pin the token: a remote trigger answers as whatever account is live at call time, so only the creator field on the response proves which account answered, and the first call after a switch still answers as the old one. And the routine API has no delete, so every create leaves a permanent row — one routine per account is kept and updated with each new job's configuration, then cleared of its connections, then run.

## Context
A real stall is told from the artefact by listing the trigger's runs: running with an advancing last-event time is alive; idle with a last-event time within a minute of creation is dead on arrival, usually a rate limit. Accounts with no external connectors are the safest seats for dispatch; the others inherit their owner's connectors unless they are cleared before the run.
