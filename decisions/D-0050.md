## Decision

Any way to make a feature cheaper or faster is filed, not just noticed. Whoever spots it — a session, the tick, the controller — writes it as an inbox card with four parts: the observation, the number it should move (minutes per landed change, dollars per feature, rounds per landing, gate minutes, tokens per session), the evidence from the record's own metrics, and how it will be proven. The groom types it, the lane builds it, and the scorecard shows the number before and after. A proposal with no number is not filed; a proposal whose number does not move is reverted and the card says so.

## Why

Operator, 2026-09-22 14:55: "whenever you spot a way to reduce time or cost of a feature by any means, suggest improvements, validate them based on data, spec and put on backlog as item for self improvement." The factory's own speed and cost is what it sells; the plan makes that the score (features on prod per week, cost per feature).

## Rules

- **Where the numbers come from**: `metrics/sessions` (tokens, minutes, cost per session), `metrics/ticks` (step durations), `metrics/daily`, the item's rounds and gate minutes. No proposal cites a number the record does not hold; if the number is missing, the first card is the measurement.
- **The bar**: a card states the expected move in percent and the measurement window (a week, or N landings). The scorecard's before/after is the evidence; D-0047's rule holds — a named test's result or a typed ruling, never a claim.
- **Reverting**: a change whose number does not move within its window is reverted by the lane and its card closed `no effect`, with the readings. That is a success of the method, not a failure.
- **Ranking**: cost/speed cards are ranked against feature work by expected saving per week, not by who asked.
- **Autonomy**: under D-0049 the factory files and ranks these itself; the operator is informed in the digest.
